Transaction Guardian: AI-Assisted Crypto Transaction Safety Layer

Timothy Dada · Team Capstone (6-person team), Cybersecurity Operations Capstone · 2026

Built for a smartwatch wallet client (name withheld) as a 6-person capstone team project. This writeup describes the system the team delivered; it was a group effort, not solo work.

Objective

Smartwatch crypto wallets ask users to approve transactions they cannot meaningfully read — a raw hex string or an unlimited-approval request, on a screen with no room for detail. As part of a 6-person capstone team, I helped build Transaction Guardian: a safety layer that computes a deterministic risk rating in code, then uses an AI model only to explain that rating in one plain-language sentence a person can act on in 2–3 seconds.

How It Works

Three stages, identical for every request type across both supported chains (Ethereum and Solana):

  • Decode — work out what a request actually does: which application, which action, how much, and who ends up with authority. Resolves proxy contracts and unwraps bundles.
  • Detect — apply 23 threat checks plus a live scam-address feed. Ordinary code, no model involved. Produces signals with a severity.
  • Explain — one model call with a fixed output shape, turning the result into a sentence a person can act on. The rating is fixed at the Detect stage; the Explain stage may raise it but never lower it, and nothing is ever signed or broadcast by the system itself.

Key Design Decision: AI Explains, Never Decides

The most consequential design choice was to keep the AI model out of the risk decision entirely. Software computes the rating; the model only writes the sentence that explains it — because a verdict has to be reproducible to be testable, and a model that reads attacker-authored content could in principle be talked down.

Limitations & Next Steps

  • Advisory, not a guarantee — a genuinely novel attack pattern may match none of the existing checks.
  • Reads intent rather than simulating transaction outcomes.
  • Two chains supported; each additional chain needs its own decoder.
  • Not yet integrated into the client's hardware signing flow; no persistence, authentication, or rate limiting yet.

Planned next: surfacing the risk badge before the explanatory sentence, hardware integration with risk-tiered authentication, and transaction simulation. The team also identified 29 further threat checks, most requiring no new data source.
